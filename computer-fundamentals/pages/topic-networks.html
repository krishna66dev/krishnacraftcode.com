Topic Explanation
Read in your preferred language

A Computer Network is a group of two or more computers (or devices) connected together to share resources — such as files, printers, internet connections, and data. Networking is the backbone of the modern internet, corporate IT infrastructure, and mobile communication.

Networks are classified by their geographic scope (LAN, MAN, WAN, PAN), their physical layout (topology — Bus, Star, Ring, Mesh), and the communication protocol they use (TCP/IP, HTTP, FTP). The OSI Model (7 layers) and TCP/IP Model (4 layers) are frameworks that define how data travels from one computer to another across a network.

Without networks, the internet would not exist. Every time you open a website, send a message, or stream a video — data is traveling through a complex web of networked devices using these protocols and models.

Types of Computer Networks
Classified by geographic coverage
🏠
PAN
Personal Area Network
Smallest network — connects personal devices within 10 meters. Examples: Bluetooth headset to phone, smartwatch to laptop, wireless keyboard.
Range: ~10 m | Tech: Bluetooth, USB, Infrared
🏢
LAN
Local Area Network
Connects computers within a single building, office, school, or campus. High speed, privately owned. Examples: School computer lab, home Wi-Fi network, office network.
Range: Up to 1 km | Tech: Ethernet, Wi-Fi
🏙️
MAN
Metropolitan Area Network
Covers a city or metropolitan area. Connects multiple LANs. Example: Cable TV network in a city, university campuses across a city, city-wide Wi-Fi.
Range: 5–50 km | Tech: Fiber optic, WiMAX
🌍
WAN
Wide Area Network
Spans countries, continents — the largest type. The Internet is the world's largest WAN. Connects multiple MANs and LANs. Uses leased lines, satellites, and fiber optic cables.
Range: Unlimited | Tech: Satellite, Leased lines, Fiber
Network Topologies
Physical arrangement of network devices
Bus
All devices share one backbone cable. Simple but single point of failure.
Star ⭐
All devices connect to a central hub/switch. Most common in LAN. Easy to manage.
Ring
Each device connects to exactly two others, forming a ring. Token passing. Failure = network down.
Mesh 🕸️
Every device connects to every other. Most reliable — no single failure point. Used in WAN, internet backbone.
Tree / Hybrid
Hierarchical combination of Bus and Star. Used in large campus/corporate networks.
Star topology is the most widely used in modern LANs. Mesh topology is used in the internet backbone and military networks for maximum reliability.
OSI Model — 7 Layers
Open Systems Interconnection — how data travels across a network
Mnemonic to remember layers (top to bottom): "All People Seem To Need Data Processing" (Application, Presentation, Session, Transport, Network, Data Link, Physical)

TCP/IP Model — 4 Layers
The practical model used by the internet today

The TCP/IP Model is the practical networking model actually used by the internet. It has 4 layers (simplified from OSI's 7). TCP/IP was developed by ARPANET (precursor to the internet) in the 1970s.

TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) ensures reliable, ordered delivery. IP (Internet Protocol) handles addressing and routing. Every device on the internet has an IP address.

OSI Layer 5+6+7 = TCP/IP Application layer. OSI Layer 1+2 = TCP/IP Network Access layer. OSI is a theoretical reference model; TCP/IP is the actual implementation used by the internet.
Network Devices
Hardware that builds and manages networks
🔌
Hub
Operates at OSI Layer 1 (Physical). Broadcasts data to ALL connected devices — no filtering. Simple but inefficient and insecure. Now mostly replaced by switches.
🔀
Switch
Operates at OSI Layer 2 (Data Link). Uses MAC addresses to send data only to the intended device — intelligent and efficient. Standard in modern LANs. Example: Cisco Catalyst, Netgear switch.
📡
Router
Operates at OSI Layer 3 (Network). Routes data packets between different networks using IP addresses. Your home Wi-Fi box is a router. Connects LAN to WAN (internet). Example: TP-Link, Cisco router.
🔁
Repeater
Operates at OSI Layer 1. Amplifies/regenerates signals that have weakened over long distances — extends network range. Used in long-distance cable networks.
🌉
Bridge
Operates at OSI Layer 2. Connects two LAN segments and filters traffic between them using MAC addresses. Reduces network congestion.
🚪
Gateway
Operates at all OSI layers. Translates between different network protocols. Acts as the entry/exit point of a network. Your router often acts as a gateway to the internet.
📶
Modem
Modulates digital data to analog signals for transmission over telephone lines and demodulates incoming analog to digital. MOdulator-DEModulator. Used for DSL/cable internet connections.
Real-Life Examples
🏫
School Computer Lab = LAN

All computers in your school lab connected to one switch/hub sharing a printer and internet — that's a LAN (Local Area Network). The switch uses Star topology. The teacher's computer acts as a server.

🌐
Opening a Website = OSI Layers in Action

You type "google.com" (Layer 7 – Application/HTTP) → SSL encrypts it (Layer 6) → Session established (Layer 5) → TCP ensures reliable delivery (Layer 4) → IP routes to Google's server (Layer 3) → Ethernet sends across your LAN (Layer 2) → Electrical signals travel through cable (Layer 1).

📱
Your Home Network

Your Router connects your home LAN to the internet (WAN). Your phone connects via Wi-Fi (Layer 2 wireless). The Modem converts ISP signals. Star topology — all devices connect to your router at the center.

Key Notes — Exam Tips
OSI = 7 layers (theoretical model). TCP/IP = 4 layers (practical internet model). OSI was developed by ISO.
Mnemonic for OSI layers (bottom-up): "Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away" (Physical, Data Link, Network, Transport, Session, Presentation, Application).
Hub = broadcasts to all (Layer 1). Switch = sends to specific MAC (Layer 2). Router = routes using IP (Layer 3).
IP address = logical address assigned to each device (e.g., 192.168.1.1). MAC address = physical hardware address burned into NIC (e.g., AA:BB:CC:DD:EE:FF).
TCP = reliable, connection-oriented (used for web, email). UDP = unreliable, connectionless, fast (used for video streaming, gaming, DNS).
Common ports: HTTP=80, HTTPS=443, FTP=21, SSH=22, SMTP=25, DNS=53, RDP=3389.
Summary — Computer Networks

Network types by range: PAN (~10m, Bluetooth) → LAN (building, Ethernet/Wi-Fi) → MAN (city, fiber) → WAN (global, internet). Topologies: Bus (shared cable), Star (central hub — most popular), Ring (token passing), Mesh (fully connected, most reliable). OSI Model: 7 layers — Physical→Data Link→Network→Transport→Session→Presentation→Application. TCP/IP: 4 layers — Network Access→Internet→Transport→Application. Devices: Hub (L1), Switch (L2, MAC), Router (L3, IP), Modem (analog↔digital). TCP = reliable; UDP = fast but unreliable.

Interview / Viva Questions
Click to reveal answers
1What is the difference between LAN, MAN, and WAN?
LAN (Local Area Network): covers a single building/campus, high speed, privately owned (e.g., school lab, home Wi-Fi). MAN (Metropolitan Area Network): covers a city, 5-50 km range (e.g., cable TV network). WAN (Wide Area Network): covers countries/globe, the internet is the largest WAN.
2Explain the OSI model and its 7 layers.
OSI (Open Systems Interconnection) model has 7 layers: 1-Physical (raw bits), 2-Data Link (MAC, framing), 3-Network (IP, routing), 4-Transport (TCP/UDP, end-to-end), 5-Session (establish/terminate sessions), 6-Presentation (encryption, format), 7-Application (HTTP, FTP — user interface). Mnemonic: "Please Do Not Throw Sausage Pizza Away."
3What is the difference between a Hub, Switch, and Router?
Hub (Layer 1): broadcasts data to ALL ports — unintelligent, creates collisions. Switch (Layer 2): uses MAC addresses to send data only to the intended device — intelligent, reduces collisions. Router (Layer 3): uses IP addresses to route data between different networks — connects LAN to WAN/internet.
4What is the difference between TCP and UDP?
TCP (Transmission Control Protocol): reliable, connection-oriented, guarantees delivery and order, slower — used for web browsing (HTTP), email (SMTP), file transfer (FTP). UDP (User Datagram Protocol): unreliable, connectionless, no delivery guarantee, faster — used for video streaming, online gaming, DNS, VoIP.
5What is the difference between IP address and MAC address?
IP Address: logical address assigned by network administrator/DHCP, can change, used for routing between networks (Layer 3). Example: 192.168.1.105. MAC Address: physical hardware address burned into the Network Interface Card (NIC) at manufacturing, permanent, used within a LAN (Layer 2). Example: AA:BB:CC:11:22:33.
