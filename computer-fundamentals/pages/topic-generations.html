Topic Explanation
Read in your preferred language

The history of computers is divided into five generations, each characterized by a significant technological advancement — particularly in the electronic component used for processing. Each generation brought smaller size, faster speed, lower cost, and greater capability.

The classification is based on the switching technology used: Vacuum Tubes (1st) → Transistors (2nd) → Integrated Circuits (3rd) → Microprocessors/VLSI (4th) → Artificial Intelligence/ULSI (5th). Understanding generations is fundamental for any CS exam.

कंप्यूटरों के इतिहास को पाँच पीढ़ियों में विभाजित किया गया है, जिनमें से प्रत्येक एक महत्वपूर्ण तकनीकी प्रगति — विशेष रूप से प्रसंस्करण के लिए उपयोग किए जाने वाले इलेक्ट्रॉनिक घटक — द्वारा चिह्नित है। प्रत्येक पीढ़ी छोटे आकार, तेज गति, कम लागत और अधिक क्षमता लेकर आई।

वर्गीकरण उपयोग की गई स्विचिंग तकनीक पर आधारित है: वैक्यूम ट्यूब (1st) → ट्रांजिस्टर (2nd) → एकीकृत परिपथ (3rd) → माइक्रोप्रोसेसर/VLSI (4th) → कृत्रिम बुद्धिमत्ता/ULSI (5th)।

1
First Generation
Vacuum Tube Computers
📅 1940 – 1956
🔦 Vacuum Tubes 🥁 Magnetic Drums 📋 Machine Language

Used vacuum tubes for switching and amplification. These computers were enormous (room-sized), consumed huge amounts of electricity, generated immense heat, and were very unreliable. Programming was done in machine language (0s and 1s) only. Input was through punched cards and paper tape. Output via printouts.

Disadvantages: Room-sized, very expensive, generated lots of heat, unreliable vacuum tubes failed frequently, no multitasking
Advantages: First electronic computers, enabled complex scientific calculations previously impossible
ENIAC (1945) EDVAC (1949) UNIVAC I (1951) IBM 701
2
Second Generation
Transistor Computers
📅 1956 – 1963
⚡ Transistors 💾 Magnetic Core Memory 📝 Assembly Language

Transistors replaced vacuum tubes — they were smaller, faster, cheaper, and more reliable. Generated much less heat. Computers became smaller (still room-sized but much reduced). Assembly language was introduced, making programming easier. High-level languages like FORTRAN and COBOL emerged. Magnetic core memory replaced drums.

Disadvantages: Still large and expensive, required air conditioning, commercial use only
Advantages: Smaller, faster, more reliable than 1st gen, less heat, assembly language programming
IBM 1401 IBM 7094 CDC 1604 UNIVAC 1108
3
Third Generation
Integrated Circuit (IC) Computers
📅 1963 – 1971
🔬 Integrated Circuits (IC) 💿 Magnetic Disk 💻 High-Level Languages

Integrated Circuits (ICs) — multiple transistors on a single silicon chip — replaced individual transistors. Computers became much smaller, faster, and cheaper. High-level languages (BASIC, Pascal, C) became widespread. Time-sharing and multi-programming OS emerged. Keyboards and monitors replaced punched cards.

Disadvantages: IC manufacturing required sophisticated technology, still relatively expensive
Advantages: Much smaller & faster, high-level languages, keyboard+monitor I/O, commercial computers for businesses
IBM System/360 PDP-8 Honeywell 6000 ICL 2900
4
Fourth Generation
Microprocessor Era
📅 1971 – Present
⚙️ Microprocessor (VLSI) 💾 Semiconductor Memory 🌐 GUI & Networking

VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration) placed millions of transistors on a single chip — the microprocessor was born. Intel 4004 (1971) was the first. Personal Computers (PCs) became available to homes. GUI (Windows, macOS) replaced command-line interfaces. Internet and networking transformed computing. Laptops, smartphones, tablets emerged.

Disadvantages: Requires sophisticated chip fabrication, heat management in high-performance chips
Advantages: Desktop/laptop/phone-sized, GUI, internet, affordable for home use, massive software ecosystem
Intel 4004 (1971) Apple II IBM PC Intel Core i-series Apple M-series
5
Fifth Generation
Artificial Intelligence Era
📅 1980s – Present & Future
🤖 AI & Machine Learning 🔬 ULSI / Nanotechnology 🗣️ Natural Language Processing

Fifth generation computers are based on Artificial Intelligence (AI) and ULSI (Ultra Large Scale Integration) — billions of transistors on a single chip. Goal: computers that can think, learn, and make decisions like humans. Technologies include: Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Natural Language Processing (NLP), voice recognition (Siri, Alexa), quantum computing research.

Challenges: Still under development, AI ethics, data privacy, high computational cost for training AI models
Advantages: Voice/face recognition, autonomous vehicles, medical diagnosis AI, language translation, quantum computing
IBM Watson ChatGPT / Claude Google DeepMind Quantum Computers NVIDIA H100 AI GPU

Key Notes — Exam Tips
ENIAC (1945) = 1st generation. UNIVAC I (1951) = first commercial computer sold to a business (US Census Bureau).
Transistor was invented at Bell Labs in 1947 by Shockley, Bardeen and Brattain (Nobel Prize 1956).
IC (Integrated Circuit) was invented by Jack Kilby (Texas Instruments, 1958) and Robert Noyce — Kilby won the Nobel Prize 2000.
Intel 4004 (1971) = world's first commercial microprocessor. 4-bit, 2300 transistors, 740 KHz.
Moore's Law (Gordon Moore, 1965): the number of transistors on a chip doubles approximately every 2 years — observed true for decades.
Speed units: Millisecond (10⁻³s) → Microsecond (10⁻⁶s) → Nanosecond (10⁻⁹s) → Picosecond (10⁻¹²s) → Femtosecond (10⁻¹⁵s).
Summary — Computer Generations

1st Gen (1940–56): Vacuum Tubes, machine language, ENIAC, UNIVAC. Room-sized. 2nd Gen (1956–63): Transistors, assembly language, IBM 1401. Less heat, more reliable. 3rd Gen (1963–71): Integrated Circuits (IC), high-level languages (FORTRAN, COBOL), IBM 360. Keyboard+monitor I/O. 4th Gen (1971–now): Microprocessors, VLSI, GUI, internet, PCs, smartphones. Intel 4004 was first microprocessor. 5th Gen (Future): AI, ULSI, NLP, machine learning, quantum computing. Goal: machine intelligence. Moore's Law: transistors double every ~2 years. Jack Kilby invented the IC.

Interview / Viva Questions
Click to reveal answers
1What technology was used in the 1st generation of computers?
Vacuum Tubes were used as switching and amplification devices. They were large, unreliable, generated lots of heat, and consumed huge power. Programming was done only in machine language (0s and 1s). Example: ENIAC (1945), UNIVAC I (1951).
2Who invented the Integrated Circuit and when?
The Integrated Circuit (IC) was invented by Jack Kilby at Texas Instruments in 1958 (and independently by Robert Noyce at Fairchild Semiconductor). Jack Kilby received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2000 for this invention. ICs led to the 3rd generation of computers.
3What is Moore's Law?
Moore's Law, stated by Gordon Moore (Intel co-founder) in 1965, predicts that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles approximately every 2 years, while the cost halves. This has held true for several decades, driving exponential improvements in computing power.
4What is VLSI and ULSI?
VLSI (Very Large Scale Integration): placing hundreds of thousands to millions of transistors on a single chip — used in 4th generation microprocessors. ULSI (Ultra Large Scale Integration): placing billions of transistors on a single chip — used in 5th generation processors. Modern Apple M3 chip has 35 billion transistors.
5What are the key features of 5th generation computers?
5th generation computers feature: AI and Machine Learning, Natural Language Processing (voice commands), parallel processing, ULSI chips with billions of transistors, quantum computing research, expert systems, neural networks, and the ability to learn and make intelligent decisions. Examples: IBM Watson, ChatGPT, Google AlphaFold, self-driving car AI.
